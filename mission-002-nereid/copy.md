# NEREID: the expedition copy

The copy on the site, chapter by chapter. It supersedes the copy in design.md and the brief. It adapts the user's second draft (below, verbatim) to the site's rules: one heading and one short callout per chapter, text that follows what the visitor is seeing, and nothing that leaves before it can be read. The user's first draft is in git history.

**Principle: controlled revelation.** Nothing is named before the visitor reaches it. The ocean is never mentioned before the breakthrough, and the chapter names in the gauge avoid spoilers too ("First Contact", not "The Ocean"). Each chapter ends on an open observation that the next one answers. The voice is a sparse documentary narrator: short declaratives, real numbers, a question at the end. Facts are real science; the expedition, its readings and its discoveries are simulated and marked so.

## The user's second draft (source)

The story needs to follow **what the audience is seeing, moment by moment.** We shouldn't reveal the ocean before they've travelled through the ice. The suspense comes from discovering what is beneath each layer.

Think of the following as narration for an immersive National Geographic expedition, with headings that carry the story forward.

### 01 — Europa
**A frozen moon with a secret beneath its surface.**
Nearly 800 million kilometres from the Sun, a small moon orbits Jupiter. From space, Europa looks like a world frozen in time.
But something about its surface has puzzled scientists for decades.

### 02 — The fracture
**Why is Europa covered in cracks?**
Long, reddish-brown fractures stretch across the moon. Scientists believe Jupiter's gravitational pull causes the ice to bend and break.
Some of these cracks may hold material from deep within Europa.
To find out more, we need to go beneath the surface.
CTA: Begin descent

### 03 — The descent
**Kilometres of ice stand between us and the unknown.**
Our robotic explorer slowly melts its way through the frozen crust.
As we descend, the sunlight disappears. The pressure increases. We rely entirely on our instruments to understand what's around us.
Then, something changes.
*Transition: Liquid water detected.*

### 04 — The ocean
**There's an entire ocean beneath us.**
We emerge from the ice into an enormous body of water.
Scientists estimate that Europa's hidden ocean could contain twice as much water as all of Earth's oceans combined.
And we're only at the top of it.
We turn on our lights and begin exploring.
CTA: Explore the ocean

### 05 — The abyss
**How do we explore an ocean we cannot see?**
Our lights illuminate only a small area. Beyond that, we have no idea what surrounds us.
We send pulses of sound through the water. As the echoes return, our instruments begin to reveal the shape of the ocean floor.
Then we notice something unusual in the temperature readings.
*Transition: Thermal anomaly detected.*

### 06 — The signal
**Something down there is giving off heat.**
In an ocean cut off from sunlight, we've detected a region of unusually warm water.
The closer we move towards the seabed, the stronger the signal becomes.
Could Europa be generating heat from beneath its ocean?
We follow the readings.
CTA: Investigate the signal

### 07 — The discovery
**We've seen this before. On Earth.**
Our lights reveal tall, rocky formations releasing clouds of mineral-rich water.
They resemble hydrothermal vents found deep in Earth's oceans.
But what makes these formations so interesting isn't just the heat.
It's what lives around them on Earth.

### 08 — The possibility
**Life doesn't always need sunlight.**
When scientists explored hydrothermal vents on Earth, they discovered entire communities of organisms surviving in complete darkness.
At the centre of these ecosystems are tiny microbes that obtain energy from chemicals released by the vents.
If life can survive under such conditions on Earth, could it have found a way to survive here too?
We move closer and collect our first water sample.
CTA: Collect sample

### 09 — The final transmission
**Could the first alien life we discover be microscopic?**
When we imagine life beyond Earth, we often picture creatures on distant planets.
But perhaps the first evidence of extraterrestrial life will be something so small that we need a microscope to see it.
That's what makes exploring Europa so important.
And it's why the search has taken us all the way to the bottom of an ocean.
CTA: Complete expedition

*An imagined expedition inspired by the real science of Europa. Mission events and discoveries are simulated.*

The key storytelling decision here is **controlled revelation**. We don't show or explain everything upfront. Each chapter ends with an unresolved observation, and the next environment reveals its meaning. The audience should feel that they are discovering Europa, rather than being taught a lesson about it.
