# NEREID: the expedition copy

The user's copy (2026-11-24). It supersedes the copy in design.md and the brief. Implemented in `index.html`; status lines and actions are synced to the visual events (see the CLAUDE.md "Current state").

A mystery that unfolds through a series of discoveries. Each discovery answers one question and raises another. The visitor is part of the expedition and discovers things at the same time as the probe.

## 00 — The Mystery
**What is hiding beneath Europa's ice?**
Scientists believe there is a vast ocean beneath this frozen moon of Jupiter. But no spacecraft has ever reached it.
Today, we're sending a probe to find out what's down there.
CTA: Begin expedition

## 01 — Arrival
**From here, it looks lifeless.**
No trees. No rivers. Just ice stretching as far as we can see.
But appearances can be deceiving. Those reddish cracks across Europa's surface could hold clues to what lies underneath.
Status: Landing site identified. Preparing probe.

## 02 — The Descent
**How far down does the ice go?**
Our probe begins melting its way through the ice. As it descends, the sunlight disappears and the pressure builds.
The instruments are still searching for water.
Status: Liquid water detected. Approaching ocean boundary.

## 03 — First Contact
**We found the ocean.**
The probe breaks through the last layer of ice. Below us is an enormous body of water, completely hidden from the Sun.
We switch on the lights. They barely reach beyond the probe.
How do we explore something we cannot see?
CTA: Activate sonar

## 04 — The Anomaly
**Wait. Why is the water getting warmer?**
Something unexpected appears in our readings. In this freezing ocean, there is a region where the water is unusually warm.
Something beneath us could be releasing heat.
We change course to investigate.
CTA: Follow the heat signal

## 05 — The Discovery
**Could this be where life begins?**
We've found an opening in the rocky ocean floor releasing warm, mineral-rich water. Scientists call this a hydrothermal vent.
On Earth, entire communities of living organisms survive around similar vents, without sunlight.
Could the same thing happen here?
Status: Collecting water samples. Analysing chemical composition.

## 06 — The Transmission
**We haven't found life. Not yet.**
But we've found water, heat and minerals — some of the ingredients that could help life survive.
We're sending our findings back to Earth.
The next mission will know exactly where to look.
CTA: Transmit findings

## The curiosity gap

| What we discover | What we make the visitor wonder |
|---|---|
| A frozen moon | What's underneath? |
| Cracks in the ice | Can we get through? |
| An enormous ocean | What's living down there? |
| An unusual heat signal | Where is it coming from? |
| A hydrothermal vent | Could life survive here? |
| Conditions potentially suitable for life | What will we discover next? |

Visuals, copy, sound and interactions are synchronised around these moments (for example, the ocean is never announced before the probe breaks through).

**Writing principles:** simple enough for children, interesting enough for adults, scientifically grounded and never unnecessarily poetic. The expedition and its discoveries are fictional, and the site says so.
